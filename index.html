Somewhere, someone is not
looking at their screen.

He sits where the air is thin and the signal does not reach, and watches the valley of lit screens below. He asks nothing of it. He is only here.

You arrive without a door to open.

He notices which hours were taken from you.

Not to scold. Not to score. He simply keeps a quiet count of where the morning went — the way snow keeps the shape of a single footprint — and offers that count to no one but you.

Hours, written in snow.

Your days laid out plainly, without colour or alarm. Glance once and they are there for you. Look away, and they ask nothing of you until you choose to return.

The mountain watches the hours pass.

It does not hurry them. It does not demand them back. You sit, or you do not — the hours are yours either way, and the mountain is patient with both.

Nothing leaves this cave.

Not your name. Not your hours. Not a single byte. What enters stays, sealed in the stone of your own machine — where no company and no cloud can reach.
