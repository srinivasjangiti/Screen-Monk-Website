02The Short Version

Your screen-time data — which applications you used, for how long, and any other activity Screen Monk records on your computer — stays on your computer. Under no circumstances is it sent to Screen Monk, to the Creator, to any third party, or to any server. The only information that ever leaves your computer is your login, which is sent to Screen Monk's authentication service so Screen Monk can verify that a real person is using the Application.

The rest of this document describes exactly what that means in practice, what rights you have over your data, and the terms on which you may use Screen Monk.

03Architecture at a Glance

Screen Monk operates on a strict separation between identity and activity. The Application processes two distinct kinds of information, and they travel to two different places.

Identity information — sent to Screen Monk

When you sign in to Screen Monk, your login (your email address and the cryptographic credential that proves you own it) is sent to Screen Monk's authentication service. This is the only category of information that leaves your computer in the course of using Screen Monk. It is used solely to verify that a real person is using the Application. It is never combined with, linked to, or used to retrieve any of your activity information.

Activity information — stays on your computer

Every other piece of information Screen Monk records — which applications you used, for how long, your window titles, your website visits, your Focus sessions, your categories and rules, your application limits, your preferences, your local application logs, and the entire contents of your Vault — stays on your computer. It is never transmitted to Screen Monk, to the Creator, to any third party, or to any server, in any form, for any purpose. There is no automatic cloud sync, no scheduled backup to a remote endpoint, and no remote mirroring of any kind.

04Information Screen Monk Processes Locally

All of the categories of information listed below are generated by Screen Monk running locally on your computer, from signals already available to a program running on your own machine. They are processed on your machine, in your Vault, and are not transmitted to anyone.

Active application and window information

The name of the foreground process (for example, the executable that produced the active window), the title of the active window, and the time at which focus changed. This is the core signal Screen Monk needs to know which application you are using.

Application and process information

The display name and file path of each distinct executable Screen Monk observes, a category you (or Screen Monk's default rules) assign to it, and an optional icon extracted from the executable. This lets Screen Monk show you a recognisable list of apps instead of a list of process names.

Usage duration

The amount of time each application was in the foreground. Screen Monk records one continuous span per (application, window title) pair, and rolls these spans up into per-day and per-hour summaries for the dashboard.

Idle and active state

Whether you are at the keyboard, derived from the Windows GetLastInputInfo API. Idle periods are recorded so Screen Monk can distinguish time you were away from your machine from time you spent using it.

System power and lock events

Whether the computer went to sleep, woke up, locked, or unlocked. Screen Monk uses these as hard boundaries on tracking so that suspended or locked time is never counted as active use.

Browser website visits (best-effort)

For common browsers, Screen Monk extracts the website domain from the active window title. Only the domain is stored; the full page title, query string, and any other URL parameters are not stored.

Focus sessions, limits, and goals

If you use the Focus feature, Screen Monk records the start time, intended duration, allow-list of apps or domains, and any distractions. If you set a daily or weekly limit on an application, the limit and the mode (soft reminder or hard block) are stored alongside the app record.

Categories, rules, and presets

Custom categories you create, title-pattern rules you define for automatic categorisation, and focus presets you save. All are stored locally so Screen Monk can apply them the next time you use the Application.

User-configured settings

Preferences you set inside the app: privacy mode, break reminder interval, launch at startup, run-in-tray, retention period, the path to your chosen Vault, the position of any floating widget windows, and similar options.

Local application logs

A short rolling log of application lifecycle events (start, stop, errors) and boot diagnostics, written to a file inside your Vault. These exist so the Application can diagnose problems on your own machine; they are not transmitted.

06How Screen Monk Uses Local Information

Information processed locally by Screen Monk is used only to provide the Application's features to you, on your machine. Concrete uses include:

  • Recording screen-time by application, window title, and website domain.
  • Displaying daily, weekly, and monthly summaries on the dashboard and Analytics view.
  • Computing per-hour and per-day timelines and "top apps" lists.
  • Categorising applications and websites into the categories you (or the default rules) assign.
  • Tracking Focus sessions, recording distractions against the session's allow-list, and storing the resulting session record.
  • Enforcing daily and weekly application limits and surfacing reminders or blocks when a limit is reached.
  • Issuing break reminders when you have been active for a sustained period, and suppressing them when you are already away from the keyboard.
  • Displaying floating widgets you have enabled, positioned where you placed them.
  • Building a recognisable icon for each application from the executable, so the dashboard is readable.
  • Producing exports you explicitly request (CSV, JSON, or a backup of your Vault), saved to a location you choose.
  • Applying the data retention policy you have configured, by pruning old activity records after the retention window has passed.

Screen Monk does not, and will not, use the information it processes locally for advertising, profiling, audience segmentation, credit scoring, employment decisions, or any other purpose outside the Application's own functionality.

07Local Storage and the Vault

Screen Monk stores the activity information it processes on your computer, in a directory called the Vault. The Vault is a normal folder; you can open it in Windows Explorer and inspect its contents at any time.

You choose where the Vault lives. The Application prompts you to pick a location during onboarding, remembers that choice, and uses it on every subsequent launch. The default location, if you do not pick one yourself, is a folder inside the Application's user data directory. You can move the Vault to a different drive, an external drive, or a cloud-synced folder at any time; doing so is a deliberate choice on your part, not something Screen Monk initiates.

Inside the Vault, Screen Monk writes:

  • A SQLite database file (managed by the open-source better-sqlite3 library) holding all activity, idle, focus, app, limit, category, rule, and summary records.
  • A folder of cached application icons extracted from the executables you have used.
  • An Exports folder containing any exports you choose to generate.
  • A Logs folder containing a short, rolling application log.
  • A small .screenmonk-vault marker file that identifies the folder as a Screen Monk Vault and stores its name and creation time.

In addition to the Vault, Screen Monk writes a small config.json file in its user data directory (managed by the open-source electron-store library). This file holds preferences such as the path to the current Vault, the position of any widget windows, and whether the Application should launch at startup. It does not hold your activity history.

Screen Monk does not, and under this Policy will not, upload, sync, mirror, replicate, or transmit the contents of the Vault or the configuration file to any server, to any third party, or to any cloud service. There is no automatic cloud sync, no scheduled backup to a remote endpoint, and no remote mirroring of any kind. If you want your Vault to be backed up off this machine, you must arrange that yourself (for example, by placing the Vault inside a folder that your own cloud-storage client already syncs).

08Network Communications and Telemetry

Outside of authentication (which is described in the next section), Screen Monk does not contain analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, error reporting, product usage tracking, advertising, remarketing, or automatic update services. The Application has no remote API of its own, and the renderer process runs under a Content Security Policy that restricts outbound network requests to the Application's own resources.

The Application may open an external link in your default web browser when you click a clickable link inside the Application. This is initiated by you, the user, and only the URL you clicked is passed to the operating system to be opened in your browser of choice. Screen Monk does not observe or record this action.

The Screen Monk website at screenmonk.app uses Google Fonts to serve the site's typography. No other third-party requests are made by the website: no analytics scripts, no advertising networks, no tracking pixels, and no social-media embeds. The website's Content Security Policy is published as a defense-in-depth measure to make this a hard guarantee, not a soft one.

09Authentication and Login

Screen Monk requires you to sign in before you can use the Application. Sign-in exists for one reason only: to verify that a real person is using Screen Monk, so that automated abuse, scraping, and free-tier farming cannot consume the Application's resources. It is not used to collect activity information, to build a profile of you, to associate you with any other user, or for any marketing purpose.

Authentication is handled by Clerk, a third-party authentication service operated by Clerk, Inc. When you sign in:

  • Your email address and the cryptographic credential that proves you own it (for example, a one-time code sent to your email, or a token issued by an identity provider you choose) are sent to Clerk's servers.
  • Clerk verifies the credential, issues a signed session token, and returns that token to the Application.
  • The Application stores the session token locally and uses it on subsequent launches to re-establish your session.

Screen Monk, the Creator, and the Application do not receive, store, log, copy, mirror, or process your password or any other authentication secret. The Creator has administrative access to the Clerk project that backs Screen Monk's authentication, and may therefore access basic Clerk account metadata (such as your email address, the date your account was created, and the date of your most recent sign-in) for the purposes of operating, securing, and supporting the Application. The Creator does not, and will not, use this access to retrieve, read, infer, or reconstruct any of your activity information, which is not held by Screen Monk and not held by Clerk.

Authentication metadata and activity metadata are strictly separated by design. There is no code path in the Application by which your screen-time data, your Vault, or your activity history can be associated with, linked to, or retrieved using the session token issued by Clerk. The session token's role is exhausted the moment it has confirmed that you are signed in.

You may sign out at any time from inside the Application. Signing out deletes the local session token. It does not delete the account record held by Clerk; you may request deletion of that record by contacting the Creator using the details in Section 25.

11Third-Party Components

Screen Monk uses a small number of third-party software components. The list below describes each one and the data it handles.

  • Clerk (Clerk, Inc.) — authentication service. Receives your email and authentication credentials at sign-in. Returns a signed session token. Holds basic account metadata. Does not receive any of your activity information.
  • Electron — the Application runtime that hosts the user interface. No network activity.
  • React — the user-interface library used to render the dashboard. No network activity.
  • better-sqlite3 — a local SQLite library used to read and write the Vault database. No network activity.
  • electron-store — a small JSON configuration store for Application preferences. No network activity.
  • koffi — a foreign-function interface used to call a small number of Windows system APIs (idle time, foreground window). No network activity.

The Screen Monk website uses Google Fonts to load the site's typography. The request to Google Fonts reveals your IP address and User-Agent to Google in the ordinary way that any web request does; this is the only request the website makes to any external service.

Screen Monk is not a data joint controller or processor on behalf of any of the above, except where the law imposes that role regardless of contract. The above components are not given access to your activity information, your Vault, or any other personal information processed locally by Screen Monk.

12User Control

Because your activity information lives on your machine, you have direct and complete control over it. The following controls are available to you.

Viewing your data

The Application's dashboard, Apps view, Browser view, Categories view, Timeline, and Analytics view let you browse the data Screen Monk has recorded. You can also open the Vault folder in Windows Explorer and inspect the underlying SQLite database directly.

Exporting your data

The Vault screen lets you export your activity data as CSV or JSON, and lets you create a local backup archive of the entire Vault. Exports and backups are written to a location you choose; they are not transmitted by Screen Monk.

Deleting your data

You can delete individual records from inside the Application, clear all data for a specific application, delete categories, rules, or focus presets, or delete the entire Vault from the Vault screen. Deleting the Vault folder is a normal folder delete in Windows Explorer and is a permanent and complete way to remove all of your activity information.

Privacy Mode and per-app overrides

A global Privacy Mode in Settings masks window titles throughout the Application. You can also set a per-app privacy override so the titles for a specific app are masked even when Privacy Mode is off. With Privacy Mode enabled, Screen Monk still records which app you used and for how long, but does not record the window title.

Retention

Settings includes a retention option. When enabled, Screen Monk prunes activity records older than the configured retention window once per day.

Uninstalling Screen Monk

The Windows uninstaller removes the Application and the default user data directory. The Vault folder you chose is not touched by the uninstaller; if you also want to remove the Vault, delete the Vault folder yourself before or after uninstalling.

Deleting your Screen Monk account

To delete the Clerk account that backs your Screen Monk sign-in, contact the Creator using the details in Section 25. The Creator will action the request within thirty (30) days. Deleting your Clerk account does not affect any activity information held locally in your Vault.

13Security

Screen Monk is designed so that the most sensitive data it handles — your screen-time history — lives on your computer, under your control, and is not transmitted to anyone. Beyond that baseline, the Application applies a number of reasonable safeguards:

  • The renderer process runs under a strict Content Security Policy that limits outbound network requests to the Application's own resources.
  • Configuration and database files include corrupt-file recovery so that a partial write cannot render the Application unusable.
  • A single-instance lock prevents the Application from being launched in two windows at once, which would otherwise race on the same database file.
  • Application lifecycle errors are logged locally; the Application surfaces a clear error message rather than failing silently.
  • Updates to Screen Monk are delivered through the official installer you downloaded; the Application does not silently self-update from a remote endpoint.
  • Authentication tokens are stored using the operating system's standard secure-storage facilities; the Application does not log tokens to disk in plaintext.

No security design is perfect. Screen Monk does not warrant that the information on your machine is immune to every possible attack. In particular, if your computer itself is compromised, or if another user with access to your computer opens the Vault, the contents of the Vault can be read. Standard operating-system-level security practices — keeping your operating system up to date, using full-disk encryption where appropriate, limiting who has access to your machine, and so on — remain the foundation on which Screen Monk's local-only design rests. You are responsible for taking those precautions.
